Cairn

User guide

Speak a thought. Your own hub writes it down.

Cairn is a pocket recorder with an e-paper screen, a microphone, a microSD card, and two buttons. Each note is saved to the card, then sent over WiFi to a hub you run, which transcribes it and files it as Markdown. This guide is for the person who carries it.

From the panel to a page

No app, no account

  1. On the device

    Press Record and speak

    Press Record again to stop, or hold it while you talk and let go. The panel counts the note’s length and shows the tag it will be filed under.

  2. On the card

    It is saved to the card

    When you stop, the note goes to the microSD card, which keeps it until the hub has it. The idle screen counts the notes still waiting, as 3 waiting.

  3. On your hub

    Your hub files it as Markdown

    Once you have tagged it, the device sends the note over WiFi, and the hub transcribes it and files it as one Markdown file, with the tag, the time, and the length in its front matter. Your notes are read there: the recorder never plays them back.

About this guide

Section titled “About this guide”

A button on the case is written as a key, as Record, and a word the panel shows is set in the panel’s own type, as Sync failed, so you can match the page to the device at a glance. The drawing at the top is the first board Cairn runs on; a board with only one button works too, as One-button boards describes. How the firmware is built and tested is in firmware/README.md, and how the hub is run is in the Cairn repository’s README.md.
