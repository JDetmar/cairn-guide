Keeping recordings

A transcript can be wrong. The hub can keep a copy of each note’s recording, so its owner can play it on the note’s page and check the note against what was really said. Keeping is off unless you turn it on, and while it is off the hub keeps only the text, as When audio is released says. Once it is on, each person chooses whether their own recordings are kept, and for how long.

Only a recording’s owner, signed in, may play it, pin it, or delete it. An admin, a reader token, a device, and every other person are refused, as if the recording did not exist. No webhook receives a recording, and nothing in the hub does anything with one but play it to its owner. You, the operator, can reach every recording on its storage, as you can read every note in the database.

The storage it needs

Section titled “The storage it needs”

The hub keeps recordings in a folder of their own, on a volume of their own. Give them a volume that is:

  • Encrypted at rest. The hub does not encrypt recordings itself, and cannot tell whether a folder is encrypted, so turn keeping on only on encrypted storage. A recording carries its owner’s voice and the voices of anyone talking near them. The spool on /data holds the same recordings until they are released, so put /data on encrypted storage too.
  • A file system of its own, mounted at the folder Cairn__Recordings__Dir names, such as /recordings, and never inside /data. Its size is the bound on what is kept: when it is full, no one’s new recording is kept until room is made, and kept recordings can never fill the disk the spool needs.
  • Sized for the recordings. The recorder sends about 1.9 MB a minute, so ten minutes a day is about 7 GB a year; the hub accepts up to 48 kHz stereo, about 11.5 MB a minute.
  • Backed up, since nothing else holds a kept recording once its spool file is released; see Backups, upgrades, and repairs.

Volumes shows where it goes beside the hub’s others. Run the hub’s commands in its own container, where the volume is mounted, since ingest-file keeps recordings too, and the recordings commands act on them.

The storage check

Section titled “The storage check”

Whenever keeping is enabled or Cairn__Recordings__Dir is set, the hub checks as it starts that the folder is set, that it exists, that it is the root of a mount, and that its mount is on a different device from the spool’s. It follows a link at any part of either path first, and two bind mounts of one disk fail the check. The hub never makes the folder, so a volume that did not mount fails the check instead of filling the container’s own disk. Where no mount table can be read, as on macOS, the check logs that it cannot tell, and passes. On a platform other than Linux and macOS it fails, since the hub keeps recordings only on those two.

A failed check is logged, the recordings health check reports it, and nothing reaches the recordings in that process: no recording is kept, no note page shows a player, the sweep does not run, and the recordings commands refuse, saying why. Notes are still filed and delivered, as they are with keeping off.

Turning it on

Section titled “Turning it on”

Set the folder, turn keeping on, and choose the default for anyone who has not chosen:

The default is dont-keep, keep, or a whole number of days from 1 to 3650, such as 30. The hub refuses to start when the folder is not an absolute path, or the default is none of these.

Keeping applies to notes transcribed after it is turned on; a note filed before was never offered, and its page says so. A change to the default applies only to recordings kept after it, so changing it never deletes anyone’s recordings.

Turning keeping off stops new keeping, and nothing more. What is already kept stays its owners’: they still play, pin, unpin, and delete their recordings and change their choice, and the sweep still deletes each when it is due. So keep the volume mounted, and Cairn__Recordings__Dir set, for as long as any recording is kept. To stop for good, turn keeping off and run recordings purge.

What each person chooses

Section titled “What each person chooses”

Each person chooses on the Recordings tab under Settings, /account/recordings, among four:

  • follow the hub’s default, which whoever runs the hub sets, and which the tab names;
  • never;
  • until they delete each one;
  • for a number of days, from 1 to 3650, a field the tab shows only with that choice.

A person may keep their own recordings even when the hub’s default is not to.

Each recording is stamped when the hub keeps it, by the hub’s own clock, never the device’s. Under a number of days, it is kept until that many whole days of 24 hours after it was kept; under keep, it has no date.

When a person changes their choice, each of their kept recordings that is not pinned is stamped again, from when it was kept, under the new choice, whether that is longer or shorter. When the new stamps would delete any recording at once, the tab says how many and asks first; a change that deletes nothing asks nothing. Choosing don’t keep, once confirmed, deletes every kept recording of theirs that is not pinned, at the sweep’s next pass, and the hub’s audit log records the change.

While keeping is off and the person has no recordings, the tab says keeping recordings is not set up on this hub. While the storage check has failed, it says the recordings’ storage cannot be reached, and offers no choice.

On the note’s page

Section titled “On the note’s page”

The note’s page has a Recording section, with a player while its recording is kept and the copy is there, and a sentence that says which of these holds:

What it says When
Kept until a date Kept, under a number of days
Kept, with no date set to delete it Kept, under keep
Pinned: kept until you delete it Pinned
Not kept: when this note arrived, your recordings were set to keep none The owner’s choice, when it was filed, was not to keep it
Not kept: the hub’s storage for recordings was full when this note arrived The volume was full
Not kept: the hub could not make its copy when this note arrived Every try at copying it failed
Not kept: this note arrived before the hub kept recordings Filed while keeping was off, so never offered
Deleted on a date Deleted, by its owner, the sweep, or the purge
Missing: the hub’s record says it is kept, but the copy is gone Its copy is not on the volume
Cannot be reached right now: the hub’s storage for recordings is not available The storage check failed

When the owner’s choice decided it, the sentence links to the Recordings tab, “Change what is kept”. A hint, “Until your webhook has the note, the hub also holds its upload to send it”, shows while the spool still holds the note’s recording.

A kept recording’s owner can:

  • pin it, which keeps it until they delete it, whatever they choose later;
  • unpin it, which stamps it again from when it was kept, under their choice now, and asks first when that would delete it at once;
  • delete it now, which asks first.

A missing recording can only be deleted, which asks first too: that clears the record that says it is kept, so integrity stops reporting it.

A deletion leaves a record that says the recording was deleted, when, and why, and then removes the copy. If removing the copy fails, the sweep removes it on its next pass. A deletion does not reach a copy the spool still holds for a delivery: that copy goes when it is released, as it always has, and is never played.

The sweep

Section titled “The sweep”

The hub sweeps the recordings once as it starts, and every hour after, as the hub acting for each person:

  • it deletes each kept recording that is due and not pinned;
  • it deletes a kept recording whose note is still not filed a day after it was kept; once it has found a recording’s note filed, it records that and never checks that recording again;
  • it removes each copy older than ten minutes whose record says deleted or not kept, as after a crash between a deletion’s two steps, or a restore of the recordings;
  • it leaves a copy with no record at all, which integrity reports and only recordings purge-unmatched deletes.

When it cannot sweep someone’s recordings, it logs why and goes on to the next person, and the recordings health check reports Degraded until a pass sweeps everyone. If no pass finishes for two hours, the health check reports that too.

When keeping fails

Section titled “When keeping fails”

The hub copies a recording just before it files the note, and reads the copy back to check it. It makes up to three tries within one deadline, 60 seconds for each 64 MiB of Cairn__MaxUploadBytes, so 60 seconds at the default. A recording whose every try fails is not kept, as the copy failed; one refused because the volume is full is not kept, for want of room, and is not tried again. Either way the note is filed and delivered as usual, the hub logs it, and the recordings health check reports Degraded for a day.

To make room, give the volume more space, or ask people to delete recordings or keep them for fewer days. A recording that was not kept is never kept later.

The recordings commands

Section titled “The recordings commands”

Both commands act on every person’s recordings, as the operator, and print what they did as JSON:

recordings purge deletes every kept recording, pinned ones included, each as its owner’s deletion would, record first and copy after. It refuses while keeping is enabled, saying “keeping is enabled; turn it off first”, so turn keeping off and restart the hub before running it. --dry-run lists how many kept recordings each person has, by user id, and deletes nothing.

recordings purge-unmatched deletes each copy older than ten minutes that has no record, such as one left by restoring the database to a moment before its record was written. --dry-run lists those copies, and deletes nothing. Run integrity first: such a copy may be the only one left of a recording someone meant to keep.

Each writes an entry in the hub’s audit log before it deletes anything; a dry run writes none. Both refuse while the storage check has failed, or no recordings folder is set, and say why. They exit 0 when done, 1 when refused, and 2 when the command line is wrong.

Checking them

Section titled “Checking them”

The integrity check also checks each person’s recordings, and reports:

Issue What it means
recording-missing A recording the hub’s record says is kept has no copy
recording-unmatched A copy of a person’s has no record
recording-unswept A copy whose record has said deleted or not kept for more than two hours, so the sweep is not removing it
recording-hash A kept copy whose bytes are not the recording the device uploaded
recording-no-account A copy under a user id with no account, or under a name the hub would never write
recording-storage A person has kept recordings, but the storage check failed

Checking the hashes reads every kept copy in full, so integrity takes longer the more is kept.

The recordings health check reports Degraded while the storage check has failed, while any recording was not kept in the last day, for want of room or as the copy failed, while the volume cannot be listed, while the sweep’s last pass could not sweep someone, or while the sweep has not finished a pass for two hours, and says when the sweep last finished; The health pages says more.

Restores, and the risk they bring

Section titled “Restores, and the risk they bring”

A deletion removes a recording from the volume, but not from the volume’s backups, which keep it until they expire. The records of deleted recordings stay in the database, so a restore of the recordings alone never serves a deleted recording again: the sweep removes each such copy.

A restore of the database to a moment before a deletion undoes it for as long as a copy of the recording remains, on the volume, in its backups, or in the spool. Keep the recordings’ backups no longer than you are willing to have a deleted recording last. Recordings and restores says what each kind of restore leaves.
